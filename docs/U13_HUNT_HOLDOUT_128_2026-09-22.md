# Hunger-aware Hunt: fresh 128-game release screen

**Prespecified balance screen: PASS.**
Kroni won **37/64 (57.8%)** with the proposed
Hunt valuation, versus **49/64 (76.6%)** with
current doctrine. Gameplay rules and Kroni's complete kit were identical.

## Design and acceptance criteria

64 matched setups per arm: eight opponents, both seats, four fresh seeds per
opponent pairing. Namespace `u13-kroni-hunt-holdout-20260922`; no earlier survey
seeds reused. Reversed seats share seeds, giving 32 opponent/seed clusters.
Both bots replan with identical weights, loadouts, flags and candidate limits.
Two processes run concurrently; each exits after one game so experimental
monkeypatches cannot enter the control arm. No mid-run tuning or seed replacement.

Criteria were recorded before execution:

- Treatment Kroni win rate between 40% and 65%: **True**.
- At least 10 percentage points lower than control: **True**
  (observed 18.8 points).
- No more than four additional round-limit finishes: **True**.
- No failed games or invalid actual submissions: **True**.

These are practical release-screen thresholds, not proof of statistical
significance or full-roster balance. Cutoff tolerance of four games was the
chosen operational definition of a material regression, not a learned value.

## Change tested

For Hunt against living Kroni, H=min(Hunger,3). Add 8H for a predicted banishment;
otherwise add 3H per predicted guard removed, capped at two guards. No bonus
for ineffective Hunts, Hunger0, dead targets, Siege or other Lords. Bonuses do
not stack. The same valuation is used for ordinary/recipe candidates and
Supplicant/artillery adjustments. Strength, outcomes, Hunger benefits, Consume,
Ravenous and feeding remain unchanged. The already-earned milestone Tear is
not treated as reversible.

## Release decision and playable validation

The prespecified screen passed. Ship the unchanged tested scoring as
`U13_COMMON_SMART_CORE_ALPHA_V29_HUNGER_HUNT` in the shared Python doctrine used
by Godot's playable bot. Retain the full Hunger system, neutral bouncing Consume,
and eleven-enemy Ravenous reward. No monster prices or other tuning changes.

This fresh sample supports the earlier doctrine finding: 15 previous Kroni
wins became losses and three losses became wins. Seat results were 19/32 and
18/32 in treatment, compared with 22/32 and 27/32 in control. Matchup variation
remains; the eight-game opponent samples do not justify individual Lord tuning.

The broader pacing goal is still unmet: only 25/64 treatment games finished
in rounds 15–20 (control 27/64). Both arms averaged about 15.2 rounds and had
eight games after round 20. Cutoffs increased from 1/64 to 3/64, within the
prespecified tolerance but still worth monitoring. This release addresses
Kroni pressure valuation, not every pacing problem.

Completed validation on the integrated release source:

- 10 focused Hunger-pressure and Kroni doctrine tests passed.
- 46 required Python release-contract tests passed.
- Integrated selected plans matched the experiment on 28 hungry-Kroni Hunt
  observations across 15 games. This checks integration fidelity, not a second
  balance sample or perfect enemy-order prediction.
- All seven native playable-tempo runners passed, followed by four exact
  Python/Godot cases comparing full worlds, events and snapshot restoration.
- Native GuardConsume, Kroni and Kroni-board suites passed.
- Shared playable planner: 23 contracts, 253 checks, zero failures.
- The broader 70-test Python run had two failing historical replay subcases
  within `test_natural_repair_and_guard_choices_preserve_keeps_in_real_combat`.
  Both identical failures were reproduced on unchanged parent source; they
  occur while applying old fixture prefixes, before the changed policy runs.
  They were not edited or counted as passing. Required release gates passed.

Six treatment candidate previews were rejected and discarded; all actual
submissions were legal. Rejected preview details remain in the evidence.

Historical experiment reproduction requires the pre-integration source named
above; the experiment installer now rejects an already-integrated checkout
rather than double-applying the bonus. The release itself uses ordinary source
functions, not runtime monkeypatches.
